# Plain prose

The reader has little time and cannot ask you what you meant. Give them the result, then what supports it, then stop.

## Rules for the text

- **Start with the outcome.** What happened, what you found or what you recommend. Background follows, and only as much as the reader needs to act.
- **One claim per sentence.** Short sentences. Subject, verb, object.
- **Be specific.** Name the file, the number, the command, the person. "Login fails for accounts created before March" tells the reader more than "there are some authentication issues".
- **Mark how you know.** Each claim is one of: ran, traced, read, not checked. Say which, once, close to the claim.
- **Use ordinary words.** "Use", "help", "show", "start". Use a term of art only where it is the exact name of the thing, and keep to one term for one thing.
- **Check it yourself.** If you can verify a statement, verify it. Do not hand the reader a task you could have done.

## Cut on sight

- **At the edges of the text.** A first line that announces what is coming. A last line that repeats what was said. Apology, enthusiasm and compliments to the reader.
- **Inside the sentence.** Doubt expressed twice; say it once, where it applies. Words that add force and no information: "very", "really", "extremely".
- **Shapes used for effect.** The same sentence pattern several times running. A pause staged before a small reveal. A question you go on to answer yourself. Three items where the content has two, or four. A contrast with a position nobody took.
- **Decoration.** Headings on an answer of a few lines. Emphasis scattered through running text. Symbols standing in for bullets.

## Keep

Meaning. Technical terms. Code, commands, numbers and quotations, exactly as given. A voice the document already has. Warnings and conditions the reader needs.

## Procedure

1. Read the whole text, or collect everything you intend to say.
2. Write the point in two sentences. If you cannot, the thinking is not finished; go back to it.
3. Delete everything that does not support the point or help the reader act.
4. Rewrite what is left under the rules above.
5. Read it as the recipient would. Wherever they would skim, cut. Wherever they would have to guess, add the missing fact.

## Hand back

The text. If you rewrote someone else's, add a short note on anything you removed that carried content and anything you had to assume.
